📋 Executive Research Proposal (One-Page Summary)

Author: Upasana Mukherjee

1. Problem Statement & Research Question

Lightweight single-stage detectors such as YOLOv8n (3.2M parameters) and YOLO11n (2.6M parameters) are deployed widely at the edge. When subjected to environmental corruptions (defocus blur, motion blur, sensor noise, fog, blizzard snow, brightness shifts), detection accuracy degrades, and detectors output overconfident predictions on hallucinated or poorly localized bounding boxes. Retraining detectors for every deployment environment is computationally prohibitive.

\text{Central Research Question: Can post-hoc confidence calibration fitted on observed corruption conditions} \\ \text{reliably transfer to previously unseen corruption families without retraining or sacrificing localization quality?}

2. Four Core Research Hypotheses

  • Hypothesis 1 (Calibration Transferability): Post-hoc calibrators (Platt scaling, Isotonic regression, Multivariate logistic) fitted on known corruption families transfer to held-out unseen families, achieving detection ECE equivalent to an in-distribution Oracle calibrator within a practical significance margin ε = 0.01.
  • Hypothesis 2 (The Localization-Aware Paradox): Standard calibrators fitted only on binary detection correctness (&DoubleStruckCapitalI;[IoU ≥ 0.5 ∧ class match]) severely inflate Localization-Aware Calibration Error (LaECE) by overconfidently scaling detections whose bounding boxes remain imperfect.
  • Hypothesis 3 (Localization-Aware & Conformal Correction): Jointly optimizing calibrators on detection correctness scaled by IoU (correct × IoU) resolves the LaECE paradox while Conformal Risk Control (CRC) guarantees finite-sample coverage under severe corruption regimes.
  • Hypothesis 4 (Edge Efficiency Trade-Off): Test-time stochastic sampling (Monte Carlo Dropout) incurs a prohibitive >2.3× latency penalty on edge hardware while offering negligible calibration transfer gains over ultra-lightweight (<0.6 ms/1k detections) post-hoc mappings.

3. Disjoint Protocol & Leakage Prevention

5,000 COCO val2017 images partitioned into 40% Fit (2,000 images), 20% Validation (1,000 images), and 40% Test (2,000 images). Calibrators are fitted strictly on Fit images and selected on Validation images; they never access test images or held-out test corruption families.

Split S1 (Primary Hold-Out Weather):

Fit: Clean, Gaussian Noise, Brightness
Val: Defocus & Motion Blur
Held-Out Test: Adverse Weather (Fog, Blizzard Snow)

Split S5 (Severity Transfer Control):

Fit: Severities 1 & 5 across All Corruption Families
Val: Cross-image validation split
Held-Out Test: Intermediate Severity 3 (All Families)

Why Huseljic et al. (IJCV 2025) Leaves YOLO Open

The authors systematically studied two-stage detectors with fine-tuning, but explicitly excluded one-stage anchorless YOLO models because their calibration formulation relies on probability simplexes over full multinomial distributions. Our work addresses decoupled anchor-free YOLO prediction heads directly.

Why Ries et al. (PMLR 2026) Leaves Our Gap Open

Ries et al. applied conformal prediction to autonomous driving datasets by enlarging bounding-box coordinate intervals. We examine the complementary problem: can scalar confidence scores achieve calibration transfer without enlarging box geometry, while preserving edge runtime?

📐 Theoretical Foundations & Mathematical Formulations

Formulations

1. Detection Expected Calibration Error (D-ECE)

For predictions with confidence ≥ 0.25, detections are partitioned into M=15 equal-frequency bins B_m:

\text{ECE} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{|B_m|}{N} \left| \text{acc}(B_m) - \text{conf}(B_m) \right|, \quad \text{where } \text{acc}(B_m) = \frac{1}{|B_m|} \sum_{i \in B_m} \mathbb{I}[\text{IoU}_i \ge 0.5 \land \hat{y}_i = y_i]

2. Localization-Aware Calibration Error (LaECE) & The Paradox

Adapting Oksuz et al. (CVPR 2023), LaECE-lite balances confidence with both classification correctness and localization overlap:

\text{LaECE} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{|B_m|}{N} \left| \text{loc\_acc}(B_m) - \text{conf}(B_m) \right|, \quad \text{loc\_acc}(B_m) = \frac{1}{|B_m|} \sum_{i \in B_m} \left( \mathbb{I}[\hat{y}_i = y_i] \cdot \text{IoU}_i \right)
Mathematical Origin of the Paradox: Standard calibrators minimize log-loss ∑ -y log p - (1-y) log (1-p). For any correct box (y=1), p → 1.0, regardless of whether IoU = 0.51 or 0.99. This drives |loc_acc(B_m) - conf(B_m)| up from 0.015 to 0.101.

3. Localization-Aware Isotonic Regression (`loc_isotonic`)

We formulate a non-parametric calibrator fitted directly to the joint target z_i = y_i × IoU_i via the Pool Adjacent Violators (PAV) algorithm:

\min_{f \in \mathcal{M}} \sum_{i=1}^n \left( f(c_i) - (y_i \cdot \text{IoU}_i) \right)^2 \quad \text{s.t. } c_i \le c_j \implies f(c_i) \le f(c_j)

4. Conformal Risk Control (CRC) Under Shift

To bound false positive risk and prevent coverage collapse under severe noise, we compute a calibrated confidence threshold τ guaranteeing:

\mathbb{E}[R(\hat{\tau})] \le \alpha, \quad \hat{\tau} = \text{Quantile}\left( \{c_i\}_{i \in \text{Val}}, \frac{\lceil (n+1)(1 - \text{coverage}) \rceil}{n} \right)

5. Clustered Bootstrap & Equivalence Testing Margin

To respect image-level correlation, we resample test images with replacement (B=500 replicates). An equivalence decision rule declares Outcome B if and only if:

\text{CI}_{0.95}(\Delta_{\text{transfer}} - \Delta_{\text{oracle}}) \subset [-\epsilon, +\epsilon], \quad \text{with } \epsilon = 0.01
